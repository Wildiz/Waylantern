# Block 1 — Academic bases for software, interfaces and embedded systems

**Status: research and structure for review; university programme verification is incomplete.**

This block covers only the 31 profiles in the software group. It does not advance the other five profession groups. The goal is to start with a published academic curriculum, preserve its structure and prerequisites, and identify relevant specialisations and study resources. The existing Waylantern practice recipes are editorial exercises; they are not the academic basis for this work.

## 1. Primary academic evidence actually read

### CS2023 — ACM / IEEE Computer Society / AAAI

Read the [official project site](https://csed.acm.org/), [final report index](https://csed.acm.org/final-report/) and [knowledge-area list](https://csed.acm.org/knowledge-areas/). The project site records endorsement dates of 18 January 2024 by ACM, 22 January 2024 by IEEE-CS and 22 February 2024 by AAAI.

Read these primary report sections, extracting their PDF text directly:

- [Executive Summary](https://csed.acm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/1.1-Executive-Summary.pdf), page 1.
- [Introduction to Knowledge Model](https://csed.acm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/1.3-Introduction-to-Knowledge-Model.pdf), especially pages 1–5.
- [Characteristics of Computer Science Graduates](https://csed.acm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2.2-Characteristics-of-Graduates.pdf), pages 1–2.

What those sections support:

- A knowledge area contains knowledge units, topics and illustrative learning outcomes. **A knowledge area is not automatically a university course.**
- The framework distinguishes **CS Core**, expected of every CS graduate, **KA Core** for deeper study, and non-core topics.
- Software Development is one representative competency area: Software Development Fundamentals, Algorithmic Foundations, Foundations of Programming Languages and Software Engineering.
- Systems Development and Applications Development are two other representative competency areas. Software Development is a prerequisite for both; mathematics/statistics and society/ethics belong across all three.
- Relevant systems subjects include architecture, operating systems, networking, parallel/distributed computing and security. Relevant application subjects include graphics, human-computer interaction, data management and specialised platforms.
- Specialised Platform Development includes web, mobile and embedded platforms with little overlap. The source explicitly warns against simply adding all their KA Core hours together.
- The representative competency areas are **not prescribed degree tracks or an exhaustive list**. Institutions select and package courses for their needs.
- The four learning-outcome skill levels are Explain, Apply, Evaluate and Develop. A programme must support demonstrable learning outcomes, rather than only list links or tools.

The report's core instructional-hour estimate is not a complete degree duration or a self-study completion estimate. This research has not yet compared individual university courses against the full body of knowledge.

## 2. Proposed families of academic bases

The following is an **editorial comparison structure to test against actual degree plans**. These are not universal admission requirements for employment, nor verified programme-to-job equivalences.

| Candidate academic base | Specialisation to investigate | Profiles to organise under it |
|---|---|---|
| Computer Science and/or Software Engineering | Application development, web frontend/backend, mobile, cloud applications, integration, software quality | Most application-development and QA profiles below |
| Computer Engineering and/or Electrical/Electronic Engineering, with the software foundations needed | Embedded systems, firmware, digital hardware and connected devices | Embedded, firmware, hardware, IoT and microsystems profiles |
| Robotics/Mechatronics or an engineering base with explicit mechanics and control content | Sensing, actuation, control and physical integration | Robotics and mechatronics profiles |
| Human-Computer Interaction, Interaction/Product Design, or a computing/design base with a documented interaction specialisation | User research, interaction, visual interface design and evaluation | UX, UI and product-design profiles |
| Computing, graphics or interactive-media/game-design programmes, depending on the work | Game programming, real-time graphics, game design, XR and testing | Game and AR/VR profiles; distinguish programming from design |

One role can have more than one relevant base. For example, a CS-based HCI track and a design-based interaction programme should be compared on their actual requirements, rather than treated as the same degree. Hardware and mechatronics cannot be reduced to an Arduino exercise.

## 4. Acceptance checklist before building a study path

For each university or educational-body programme, record:

1. Exact institution, department, programme title and qualification level.
2. Catalogue/cohort year, source URL and date inspected.
3. Required courses, elective choices and prerequisites taken from the source.
4. Credit/workload system and laboratory or capstone requirements, without equating systems automatically across countries.
5. Explicit specialisations/tracks offered by the source, separate from our proposed profession links.
6. Learning resources mapped to those requirements, with coverage gaps shown.

The next deliverable for this block is a comparison of accepted US and Chinese programme plans and their documented specialisations. The proposed family table above should be reviewed alongside those sources. No second profession group has been started.
