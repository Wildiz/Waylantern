# Learning curriculum: Scrum Master

Study Scrum responsibilities and practise facilitation using a small team case.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Leadership, product and technology relationships](professions-leadership.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Scrum Master](professions-leadership.md#scrum-master)

**Starting point:** Learn the stated foundations first

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Has observed a collaborative project and can distinguish a delivery task from a team-process problem.
- Can facilitate a short discussion and record agreed actions.

**What you will produce:** A framework study log, facilitated retrospective and a measured impediment-improvement experiment.

**Resource coverage:** 1 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Study the framework before using its labels

**Learn:**

- Scrum accountabilities
- Events and artefacts
- Commitments

**Resource still needed:** A verified Scrum Guide and Scrum-specific learning source have not yet been added; this required framework study remains a resource gap.

**Practise:** Read a verified current Scrum Guide when it is added to the catalogue. Build a concept map linking accountabilities, events, artefacts and commitments; mark unanswered questions.

**Advance when:** You can explain the map without equating Scrum Master with project manager or assigning work to team members.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Observe a team and separate symptoms from causes

**Learn:**

- Working agreements
- Impediments
- Team observations

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).

**Practise:** Use a small project you can observe, or a clearly labelled case. Record three examples of blocked collaboration and select one impediment within the team influence.

**Advance when:** The record contains observations, a hypothesis and a bounded impediment rather than a judgement about a person.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Facilitate one improvement conversation

**Learn:**

- Participation
- Retrospective structure
- Action ownership

**Resource still needed:** The catalogue lacks a verified Scrum facilitation curriculum; the exercise requires practice and feedback, not just reading a tool reference.

**Practise:** Prepare and facilitate a short retrospective with willing participants. Make room for different views, select one action and record its owner and review point.

**Advance when:** Participants can describe the agreed action and how they will inspect its effect; confidential discussion is not published as training data.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Inspect the effect instead of counting ceremonies

**Learn:**

- Improvement measures
- Experiment review
- Facilitation feedback

**Resource still needed:** Verified learning material on Scrum effectiveness and coaching feedback is still needed.

**Practise:** Try the action over an agreed work interval, gather evidence and ask for feedback on the facilitation. Write whether to keep, change or stop the experiment.

**Advance when:** Your case contains a framework map, observation log, action and review evidence, and distinguishes team improvement from merely holding a meeting.

## After this recipe

Repeat supervised facilitation with a real team and resolve the Scrum-source gap before presenting this as a complete Scrum learning programme.
