# Learning curriculum: Incident Manager

Coordinate restoration of a service while keeping impact, ownership and communications clear.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Operations, platforms and IT support](professions-operations.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Incident Manager](professions-operations.md#incident-manager)

**Starting point:** Prior practical experience required

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Have hands-on support or operational incident experience.
- Understand escalation ownership and service-impact communication.

**What you will produce:** A tabletop incident timeline with decisions, user updates, recovery checks and a handover.

**Resource coverage:** 1 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

## 1. Classify a reported interruption

**Learn:**

- Impact and urgency
- Initial evidence and ownership

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [GLPI](https://github.com/glpi-project/glpi) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-it-support.md#resource-glpi).

**Practise:** Enter a fictional service interruption and record users affected, known symptoms and the responsible technical team.

**Advance when:** Separate verified impact from an estimated scope and name the current incident owner.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Coordinate restoration work

**Learn:**

- Task ownership
- Workarounds and escalation

**Resource still needed:** No reviewed incident-coordination or service-restoration curriculum is catalogued; the workflow is an editorial tabletop.

**Practise:** Assign diagnostic and recovery tasks in a fictional scenario, including a condition for escalating unresolved work.

**Advance when:** Each action has one owner and expected evidence, with no competing untracked recovery changes.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Communicate verified progress

**Learn:**

- Status cadence
- User-facing uncertainty

**Resource still needed:** A reviewed operational communications guide and organisation-specific notification policy are missing.

**Practise:** Write an initial notice and two updates as new facts arrive, avoiding guessed causes or unsupported restoration times.

**Advance when:** Updates state impact, work underway, next update time and what remains unknown.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Capstone: confirm restoration and hand over

**Learn:**

- Functional recovery checks
- Timeline and follow-up

**Resource still needed:** This editorial simulation is not an official incident-management process or a substitute for supervised operational practice.

**Practise:** Run a tabletop from first report to recovery and close it only after the fictional service test and stakeholder confirmation.

**Advance when:** The record distinguishes service restoration from root-cause analysis and assigns follow-up work.

## After this recipe

Participate in real incidents under an experienced coordinator and learn the organisation's escalation, communication and review policies.
