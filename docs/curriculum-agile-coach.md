# Learning curriculum: Agile Coach

Diagnose a collaboration problem, choose an intervention and inspect organisational effects.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Leadership, product and technology relationships](professions-leadership.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Agile Coach](professions-leadership.md#agile-coach)

**Starting point:** Prior practical experience required

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Has practical delivery-team experience and can describe more than one working approach.
- Has facilitated team discussions and received feedback on that facilitation.
- Can recognise the limits of personal authority and escalate employment or wellbeing concerns appropriately.

**What you will produce:** An evidence-based coaching agreement and a reviewed improvement experiment across team boundaries.

**Resource coverage:** 1 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Establish the coaching problem and boundaries

**Learn:**

- Coaching agreement
- Stakeholder goals
- Observation

**Resource still needed:** No verified agile-coaching curriculum or professional coaching ethics source is currently catalogued.

**Practise:** Use a real consenting team context or a labelled case. Agree what problem to investigate, who is involved, what evidence can be used and what decisions are outside the coaching remit.

**Advance when:** The agreement separates the desired outcome, sponsor expectations, confidentiality boundaries and the coach influence.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Understand the system rather than prescribe a ritual

**Learn:**

- Work flow
- Dependencies
- Feedback loops

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).

**Practise:** Map how a small item moves from request to feedback across two teams. Record queues, dependencies and conflicting goals; validate the map with participants when possible.

**Advance when:** A participant can follow one item through the map and correct it, and the proposed problem is supported by observations.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Choose a reversible intervention

**Learn:**

- Facilitation versus coaching
- Experiment scope
- Unintended effects

**Resource still needed:** A verified curriculum on coaching approaches and organisational change is needed; product workshop material alone does not cover this profession.

**Practise:** Compare two interventions for one bottleneck, including the option to change nothing. Define a small reversible trial, a leading measure and a possible adverse effect to watch.

**Advance when:** The selected intervention has a rationale, owner, review date and stop condition; it is not justified only by adherence to a named method.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Review the intervention with the system

**Learn:**

- Evidence review
- Stakeholder feedback
- Capability transfer

**Resource still needed:** Verified material on organisational coaching evaluation and sustainable capability development is still missing.

**Practise:** Run the bounded trial with consent and supervision, or produce a simulated evidence packet clearly marked as such. Review outcomes and hand over the inspection process to the team.

**Advance when:** The final case explains initial evidence, intervention choice, effects, limitations and how the team will continue without depending on the coach.

## After this recipe

Build a supervised case portfolio across different team contexts; do not treat a simulated workshop as evidence of organisational coaching experience.
