# Learning curriculum: Telecommunications Specialist

Document and troubleshoot a communications service while distinguishing service symptoms from equipment assumptions.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Operations, platforms and IT support](professions-operations.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Telecommunications Specialist](professions-operations.md#telecommunications-specialist)

**Starting point:** Learn the stated foundations first

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Understand basic IP connectivity.
- Use only authorised communication systems and isolated practice equipment.

**What you will produce:** A service inventory, symptom-based diagnostic checklist and fictional escalation record.

**Resource coverage:** 2 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Map service components

**Learn:**

- Voice and data service boundaries
- Provider and customer responsibility

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Computer Networks: A Systems Approach](https://github.com/SystemsApproach/book) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-networking.md#resource-computer-networks-systems-approach).

**Practise:** Draw a hypothetical service from an end device through local connectivity to a provider handoff.

**Advance when:** Identify which elements an operator controls and which require escalation to the provider.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Learn physical checks and safe limits

**Learn:**

- Cabling and connectors
- Power and equipment status

**Resource still needed:** A reviewed telecommunications installation, cabling and electrical-safety manual is missing.

**Practise:** Create an equipment inspection checklist using photographs or fictional inventory; mark tasks requiring specialist safety guidance.

**Advance when:** The checklist does not ask a learner to open powered equipment or modify carrier infrastructure.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Investigate service quality

**Learn:**

- Latency, loss and name resolution
- Evidence collection

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Mininet](https://github.com/mininet/mininet) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-networking.md#resource-mininet).
- [Wireshark](https://www.wireshark.org/) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-networking.md#resource-wireshark).

**Practise:** Compare normal and impaired lab traffic and record timing, packet loss and the service symptom.

**Advance when:** A short report separates measured IP behaviour from untested explanations about a real voice or access network.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Capstone: escalate a reproducible fault

**Learn:**

- Service-impact summary
- Ownership and verification

**Resource still needed:** The catalogue lacks a reviewed end-to-end telecommunications support lab; this scenario covers diagnosis and communication only.

**Practise:** Write a fictional outage ticket with the observed symptom, safe checks, relevant evidence and a handoff to the responsible team.

**Advance when:** A receiving operator knows the affected service, what was checked and what remains to be measured.

## After this recipe

Choose a technology such as enterprise voice, fibre access or radio and obtain supervised practice with its current manuals and safety rules.
