# Learning curriculum: Engineering Manager

Practise team planning, feedback, technical coordination and delivery review.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Leadership, product and technology relationships](professions-leadership.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Engineering Manager](professions-leadership.md#engineering-manager)

**Starting point:** Prior practical experience required

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Has contributed to an engineering team and understands the software delivery lifecycle.
- Can discuss implementation tradeoffs and has experience supporting colleagues.

**What you will produce:** A team operating and delivery case with feedback practices, accountable decisions and a reviewed outcome.

**Resource coverage:** 2 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

## Your sequence

1. [Understand the team work and constraints](#stage-1)
2. [Learn people-management responsibilities](#stage-2)
3. [Coordinate a delivery and technical decision](#stage-3)
4. [Review both delivery and team sustainability](#stage-4)

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Understand the team work and constraints

**Learn:**

- Team purpose
- Delivery flow
- Skills and support needs

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).

**Practise:** Map a small team work flow and discuss one obstacle with willing participants. Separate delivery constraints, technical uncertainty and support needs instead of attributing every delay to performance.

**Advance when:** The case identifies a team outcome, observed bottleneck and assumptions that need further discussion.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Learn people-management responsibilities

**Learn:**

- Feedback
- Coaching boundaries
- Fair performance processes

**Resource still needed:** Verified people-management, employment-practice and manager-coaching curricula are not yet catalogued; technical references cannot substitute for them.

**Practise:** Draft a constructive feedback conversation using a fictional scenario. Identify what belongs in coaching, what requires an organisational process and when qualified HR advice is needed.

**Advance when:** The draft uses observable behaviour and a clear support request, and does not turn a training role-play into an employment decision.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Coordinate a delivery and technical decision

**Learn:**

- Scope negotiation
- Technical ownership
- Risk communication

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Markdown Architectural Decision Records (MADR)](https://github.com/adr/madr) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-architecture.md#resource-madr).

**Practise:** For a practice service change, agree a bounded outcome, owners and a decision record for a relevant tradeoff. Explain the delivery risk to a non-specialist stakeholder.

**Advance when:** The team plan identifies scope, owners and a review point, while the technical record shows engineer input rather than an imposed implementation.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Review both delivery and team sustainability

**Learn:**

- Outcome evidence
- Learning review
- Capability development

**Resource still needed:** A verified engineering-management curriculum and supervised responsibility for real team development remain necessary.

**Practise:** Run a small collaborative project or analyse a labelled case. Review the delivered outcome, coordination failures and a development need, then choose one improvement with follow-up evidence.

**Advance when:** Your capstone includes a team purpose, people-support exercise, delivery decision and review, with clear limits on what a simulation demonstrates.

## After this recipe

Seek supervised management responsibility and regular feedback from engineers and stakeholders; develop people skills alongside technical judgement.
