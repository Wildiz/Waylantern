# Learning curriculum: Audit Manager

An experienced-practitioner recipe for planning assurance engagements, supervising workpapers and managing follow-up quality.

**Target profile:** [Audit Manager](professions-security.md#audit-manager)

**Starting point:** Prior practical experience required

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Experience preparing and reviewing audit workpapers.
- Knowledge of audit independence, evidence evaluation and stakeholder communication.
- Experience coordinating people or an audit engagement under supervision.

**What you will produce:** A mock engagement-management pack with scope, resource plan, review decisions and a follow-up dashboard.

**Resource coverage:** 0 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

## 1. Set the engagement strategy

**Learn:**

- Risk-based audit objectives
- Independence, scope and stakeholder expectations

**Resource still needed:** No professional audit-management curriculum or governing audit standard has been verified in the catalogue.

**Practise:** Draft a fictional technology audit charter and engagement scope, including conflicts of interest and explicit exclusions.

**Advance when:** The plan distinguishes the audit objective from management's responsibilities and defines who can approve scope changes.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Plan resources and review gates

**Learn:**

- Skills coverage, effort and dependencies
- Sampling oversight and evidence standards

**Resource still needed:** No verified staffing benchmark or audit quality framework is provided; the work plan is an editorial simulation.

**Practise:** Build a work plan for three fictional auditors with different skills and schedule review gates before conclusions are issued.

**Advance when:** The plan identifies expertise gaps, dependencies and how review quality will be checked.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Review and challenge findings

**Learn:**

- Evidence sufficiency and consistency
- Disputed findings and escalation

**Resource still needed:** The current resources do not verify a standard-specific assurance review process; use qualified supervision for real engagements.

**Practise:** Review a synthetic packet with a supported finding, an unsupported claim and contradictory records; write review notes and adjudication steps.

**Advance when:** The review log explains which conclusions can stand, which need more work and how disagreement is handled independently.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Deliver and monitor the engagement

**Learn:**

- Executive reporting and accountable actions
- Follow-up, closure and lessons learned

**Resource still needed:** This editorial management pack does not confer authority to issue a professional assurance opinion.

**Practise:** Prepare an audit committee-style summary, tracked remediation actions and a retrospective about missed assumptions.

**Advance when:** A reviewer can trace priorities to evidence and see closure criteria, owners and unresolved management decisions.

## After this recipe

Validate the management pack with an experienced audit leader and learn the organization's governing standards before leading an engagement.
