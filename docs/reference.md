## Pros and cons

<details><summary>Show pros and cons for each entry</summary>

**ADDitude**

- Good: Large free library of articles, free webinars and downloads
- Good: Sections on adults, time management and productivity
- Watch out: Commercial publication with sponsored content
- Watch out: General information only, not medical advice
- Watch out: Not specific to learning to code

**Awesome (sindresorhus/awesome)**

- Good: Entry point to hundreds of topic-specific curated lists
- Good: CC0 licensed
- Watch out: Only an index: quality and freshness differ per linked list
- Watch out: Pull requests temporarily disabled according to the README

**Awesome Data Science**

- Good: Has a "where do I start" section
- Good: Covers courses, tools and datasets in one place
- Watch out: Long list; needs a goal to filter
- Watch out: Some linked courses may be paid

**Awesome Machine Learning**

- Good: Compare ML libraries across languages
- Good: Actively maintained
- Watch out: Tools list, not a learning path
- Watch out: Maintainer limits pull requests due to LLM-generated submissions (README, April 2026)

**Awesome Python**

- Good: Quickly find the standard tool for a task
- Good: Very active repo
- Watch out: Tool list, not a tutorial
- Watch out: Opinionated selection

**Awesome-Selfhosted**

- Good: Hundreds of free-software projects grouped by use case
- Good: Good way to learn Linux, Docker and networking by running real services
- Watch out: A list, not a course
- Watch out: You need your own hardware or server and time to maintain it

**Big-O Cheat Sheet**

- Good: Quick lookup before interviews
- Good: Single page, downloadable as PDF
- Watch out: Reference only: does not explain how algorithms work
- Watch out: Single author's page; no visible update log checked

**Build Your Own X**

- Good: Wide range of topics and languages
- Good: Learn by building
- Watch out: Link quality and freshness vary by tutorial
- Watch out: Not a course; each linked tutorial has its own terms

**Cal Newport: essays and Study Hacks blog**

- Good: Free essays and newsletter
- Good: Written by a CS academic with a developer-friendly angle
- Watch out: Not ADHD-specific
- Watch out: Books, courses and some media are paid or on a separate site

**CHADD**

- Good: Nonprofit with free educational pages and a National Resource Center line
- Good: Recursos en espanol section and Spanish-speaking helpline option
- Good: Online support communities

- Watch out: Membership, magazine and some content are paid
- Watch out: General information only, not medical advice

**CSS-Tricks**

- Good: Articles, Guides and an Almanac of CSS properties
- Good: Free to read
- Good: Actively publishing (articles dated Aug-Sep 2026 seen)
- Watch out: Article collection rather than a structured course
- Watch out: Quality and depth vary by author

**DevDocs**

- Good: Single search across many docs
- Good: Works offline
- Good: Open source (freeCodeCamp/devdocs)
- Watch out: Documentation only, not tutorials
- Watch out: Docs are in English

**Devhints**

- Watch out: Single-author collection

**free-programming-books**
