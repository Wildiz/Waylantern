# Learning curriculum: Chief Technology Officer

Connect technology capabilities, architecture and organisational choices to a product strategy.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Leadership, product and technology relationships](professions-leadership.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Chief Technology Officer](professions-leadership.md#chief-technology-officer)

**Starting point:** Prior practical experience required

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Has led engineering work through delivery and operation of production systems.
- Can evaluate architecture tradeoffs and work with product, finance and security stakeholders.
- Has experience developing people and technical capability beyond personal implementation.

**What you will produce:** A challenged technology strategy with architecture options, capability needs and inspection measures.

**Resource coverage:** 2 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Describe the technology problem in product terms

**Learn:**

- Product strategy
- Technical constraints
- Capability assessment

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [arc42 Architecture Documentation Template](https://github.com/arc42/arc42-template) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-architecture.md#resource-arc42-template).

**Practise:** Use an existing product case to document quality goals, constraints and system context. Identify which technical limits affect product outcomes rather than listing fashionable technologies.

**Advance when:** The brief connects at least two product objectives to current technical constraints and distinguishes measured facts from assumptions.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Evaluate strategic technical options

**Learn:**

- Architecture options
- Build versus buy
- Reversibility

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [The System Design Primer](https://github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-cs.md#resource-system-design-primer).
- [Markdown Architectural Decision Records (MADR)](https://github.com/adr/madr) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-architecture.md#resource-madr).

**Practise:** Compare two plausible directions for one system capability. Record workload assumptions, operational demands, migration costs and reversible decisions using an architecture decision record.

**Advance when:** Reviewers can understand why one option fits the stated requirements and what evidence would justify changing direction.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Plan the organisational capability to deliver

**Learn:**

- Team capability
- Investment tradeoffs
- Technology governance

**Resource still needed:** Verified resources on executive technology finance, organisational design and leadership development are not yet catalogued.

**Practise:** Map the proposed direction to skills, ownership boundaries, hiring or development needs and budget assumptions. Compare delivery scenarios with product and operational stakeholders.

**Advance when:** The proposal names accountable capabilities and constraints rather than assuming that a new tool or new hire resolves the strategy.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Defend and review a technology roadmap

**Learn:**

- Portfolio sequencing
- Migration risk
- Strategy feedback

**Resource still needed:** A verified CTO curriculum and real cross-functional leadership practice remain necessary beyond the technical references in this catalogue.

**Practise:** Produce a roadmap with an initial validation milestone, migration dependencies and review measures. Ask experienced reviewers to challenge both the technical and organisational assumptions.

**Advance when:** Your capstone includes a product-linked brief, option decision, capability plan and roadmap, plus a documented response to critical feedback.

## After this recipe

Build evidence through accountable engineering leadership and production outcomes; keep revising strategic decisions as product conditions change.
