# Learning curriculum: Product Owner

Translate a product goal into ordered work and validate a small delivered increment.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Leadership, product and technology relationships](professions-leadership.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Product Owner](professions-leadership.md#product-owner)

**Starting point:** Learn the stated foundations first

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Can explain a digital product and collaborate on a small project.
- Understands that a Product Owner role depends on the team context; Scrum rules require separate study.

**What you will produce:** A coherent product goal, ordered backlog, acceptance examples and a reviewed increment.

**Resource coverage:** 2 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Clarify one product goal

**Learn:**

- User value
- Product goal
- Constraints

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).

**Practise:** For a small study-resource product, write the user problem, an outcome goal and two constraints. Record which stakeholder can clarify each assumption.

**Advance when:** The goal describes a change for users rather than a list of features, and the assumptions have named questions.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Turn the goal into ordered work

**Learn:**

- Backlog ordering
- Small work items
- Acceptance examples

**Resource still needed:** The catalogue does not yet contain a verified Product Owner or Scrum curriculum covering accountabilities and backlog management.

**Practise:** Create five small backlog items supporting the goal. Add concrete acceptance examples and order them with a value, risk or learning rationale.

**Advance when:** Every top item has an observable acceptance example and its priority is explained relative to the same product goal.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Clarify work with the people doing it

**Learn:**

- Refinement
- Dependencies
- Scope negotiation

**Resource still needed:** A verified curriculum for product-backlog refinement and delivery-team collaboration is still needed.

**Practise:** Walk through the first two items with a collaborator, or simulate the questions and label the simulation. Split an oversized item and record a clarification without prescribing implementation details.

**Advance when:** The two items can be explained consistently, dependencies are recorded and one scope decision is traceable.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Review an increment and adapt the backlog

**Learn:**

- Acceptance evidence
- Feedback
- Next ordering decision

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [GitHub Skills: Introduction to GitHub](https://github.com/skills/introduction-to-github) — beginner in this topic; free access. [Details](area-software.md#resource-github-skills-introduction-to-github).

**Practise:** Use an existing small prototype or practice repository to demonstrate one completed item. Compare its result with the acceptance examples, record feedback and reorder the next items.

**Advance when:** Your packet contains the goal, backlog, a demonstrated increment, acceptance evidence and a documented change following feedback; merely closing a ticket is not treated as value delivered.

## After this recipe

Study the applicable official role framework when verified sources become available, then practise with a delivery team under supervision.
