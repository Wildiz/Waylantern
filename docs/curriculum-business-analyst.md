# Learning curriculum: Business Analyst

An editorial discovery and requirements recipe, with business-analysis methods training gaps clearly identified.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Data, analytics and artificial intelligence](professions-data-ai.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Business Analyst](professions-data-ai.md#business-analyst)

**Starting point:** Learn the stated foundations first

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Clear written communication
- Basic understanding of an organizational process
- Ability to distinguish observations from assumptions

**What you will produce:** A small requirements package containing a process description, stakeholder needs and testable acceptance examples.

**Resource coverage:** 1 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

## Your sequence

1. [Describe the current process and problem](#stage-1)
2. [Elicit and reconcile needs](#stage-2)

4. [Capstone - review a bounded change proposal](#stage-4)

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Describe the current process and problem

**Learn:**

- Stakeholders and process boundaries
- Observations versus assumptions
- Desired outcomes and constraints

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).

**Resource still needed:** This practice library offers related activities, not a complete verified business-analysis competency curriculum.

**Practise:** Observe or simulate a simple process such as booking a shared room and record handoffs, delays and unresolved questions.

**Advance when:** Separate a reported symptom from an assumed solution and identify the people affected by the process.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Elicit and reconcile needs

**Learn:**

- Interview and workshop questions
- Conflicting stakeholder needs
- Scope and priorities

**Resource still needed:** No verified requirements-elicitation or business-analysis methods course is currently catalogued.

**Practise:** Conduct a permitted small discovery exercise or use an explicitly simulated case, recording needs and conflicting priorities.

**Advance when:** Every proposed requirement traces to an observation or stated need, and disagreements remain visible rather than being silently resolved.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Specify behavior and acceptance examples

**Learn:**

- Functional behavior and quality constraints
- Alternative and failure cases
- Traceable acceptance criteria

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [arc42 Architecture Documentation Template](https://github.com/arc42/arc42-template) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-architecture.md#resource-arc42-template).
- [Markdown Architectural Decision Records (MADR)](https://github.com/adr/madr) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-architecture.md#resource-madr).

**Resource still needed:** Architecture templates support documenting decisions and constraints but do not replace specialist requirements-engineering instruction.

**Practise:** Write a small set of requirements with concrete examples for the normal booking flow, a conflict and an invalid request.

**Advance when:** Another reader can judge whether each example passes or fails without interpreting vague words such as fast or intuitive.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Capstone - review a bounded change proposal

**Learn:**

- Problem and option comparison
- Requirements traceability
- Acceptance and remaining questions

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).

**Practise:** Package the current process, needs, proposed change, acceptance examples and assumptions in a short reviewable document.

**Advance when:** A reviewer can trace the proposal to evidence, challenge an assumption and identify what must be decided before implementation.

## After this recipe

Add verified business-analysis and requirements methods plus real domain practice; this simulated package does not constitute a professional standard.
