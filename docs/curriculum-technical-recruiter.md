# Learning curriculum: Technical Recruiter

Translate a hiring need into an evidence-based role brief and a structured recruitment workflow.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Leadership, product and technology relationships](professions-leadership.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Technical Recruiter](professions-leadership.md#technical-recruiter)

**Starting point:** Learn the stated foundations first

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Can describe basic technology roles and communicate clearly with hiring stakeholders.
- Understands that employment law and organisational hiring policies need qualified local guidance.

**What you will produce:** A fictional hiring brief, structured assessment plan and documented candidate communication workflow.

**Resource coverage:** 1 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Clarify the hiring problem with the team

**Learn:**

- Role outcomes
- Essential versus learnable skills
- Seniority expectations

**Resource still needed:** No verified technical-recruiting curriculum or applicable employment-practice source is currently catalogued.

**Practise:** Use a fictional technical team and ask what work the new person must perform. Draft a role brief separating required outcomes, essential constraints, learnable skills and unsupported wish-list items.

**Advance when:** The brief links every essential criterion to actual work and distinguishes seniority from a list of technologies.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Understand candidate preparation without copying an interview script

**Learn:**

- Technical vocabulary
- Assessment relevance
- Candidate context

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Tech Interview Handbook](https://github.com/yangshun/tech-interview-handbook) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-cs.md#resource-tech-interview-handbook).

**Practise:** Read the candidate-facing interview material to understand its vocabulary and preparation demands. Draft questions for the hiring team about how each proposed assessment relates to the job; do not treat the handbook as a validated hiring rubric.

**Advance when:** Your plan explains which job evidence each assessment seeks and identifies an irrelevant or duplicative burden to remove.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Design a structured and respectful workflow

**Learn:**

- Consistent criteria
- Candidate communication
- Information handling

**Resource still needed:** Verified material on fair selection, local employment requirements and recruitment data handling remains a gap.

**Practise:** For invented candidates, outline stages, responsible reviewers and a consistent evidence rubric. Write invitation, status and outcome templates and identify where accommodation or legal advice is needed.

**Advance when:** The workflow states what information is needed, who reviews it and when candidates receive an update, without making decisions about real applicants.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Review a simulated hiring case

**Learn:**

- Evidence calibration
- Process bottlenecks
- Feedback

**Resource still needed:** A verified recruiting-practice curriculum and supervised organisational experience are necessary before using the exercise as an operational hiring process.

**Practise:** Run a tabletop review with two fictional candidate packets. Compare how reviewers apply the same criteria, identify inconsistent reasoning and revise the process with a documented rationale.

**Advance when:** The capstone contains the brief, workflow, evidence rubric, communication templates and a review showing one corrected inconsistency.

## After this recipe

Seek supervised recruiting experience with qualified HR and technical reviewers; add jurisdiction-appropriate professional guidance rather than infer it from programming resources.
