# Learning curriculum: Chief Information Security Officer

An experienced-leader recipe for connecting security strategy to business risk, governance, investment and incident decisions.

**Target profile:** [Chief Information Security Officer](professions-security.md#chief-information-security-officer)

**Starting point:** Prior practical experience required

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Substantial experience leading security or risk work.
- Experience managing stakeholders, budgets and people.
- Familiarity with incident leadership, assurance and business accountability.
- Access to experienced executive or security-leadership feedback.

**What you will produce:** A simulated board-level security strategy with prioritized investments, accountable risk decisions and a crisis exercise review.

**Resource coverage:** 0 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Understand mandate and business exposure

**Learn:**

- Business strategy, critical services and dependencies
- Governance, accountability and risk ownership

**Resource still needed:** No verified CISO curriculum or organization-specific governance model is supplied; this recipe presupposes professional experience.

**Practise:** Draft a fictional CISO mandate and map critical services, decision owners and existing assurance evidence.

**Advance when:** The mandate distinguishes advice, operational responsibility and business risk acceptance without assuming unlimited authority.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Build a risk-informed investment strategy

**Learn:**

- Portfolio priorities and resource trade-offs
- People, technology and measurable outcomes

**Resource still needed:** No leadership programme, budget benchmark or universal security maturity target has been verified; figures are simulated.

**Practise:** Propose three competing security investments for a constrained fictional budget and document the consequences of deferring each.

**Advance when:** The proposal connects each investment to business exposure, ownership, cost assumptions and observable outcomes.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Exercise executive incident decisions

**Learn:**

- Crisis coordination and business continuity
- Disclosure dependencies and authorized communication

**Resource still needed:** Jurisdiction-specific disclosure duties and crisis-management standards remain unverified and must be checked by qualified specialists.

**Practise:** Run a tabletop for a fictional disruptive incident with a decision log, business-impact estimates and questions for legal and communications specialists.

**Advance when:** The log records decision rights and evidence limitations; it does not invent notification deadlines or bypass qualified advice.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Present strategy and assurance to leadership

**Learn:**

- Board communication and residual risk
- Review cadence and independent challenge

**Resource still needed:** An editorial board-pack exercise is not a substitute for CISO experience, an executive appointment or professional qualification.

**Practise:** Deliver the mandate, investment proposal and tabletop findings as a short board pack; obtain a critical review from an experienced leader.

**Advance when:** The pack identifies requested decisions, residual risk, accountable owners, assurance gaps and when progress will be reassessed.

## After this recipe

Use experienced executive mentoring and real organizational governance to validate the strategy; verify current obligations and source material before adopting any decisions.
