# Learning curriculum: Product Manager

Investigate a user problem, prioritise an outcome and evaluate a small product experiment.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Leadership, product and technology relationships](professions-leadership.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Product Manager](professions-leadership.md#product-manager)

**Starting point:** Learn the stated foundations first

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Can describe a digital service and its users in plain language.
- Can organise a small table and distinguish an observation from an assumption.

**What you will produce:** A product discovery brief, prioritised options and an experiment report with a justified next decision.

**Resource coverage:** 3 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

## Your sequence

1. [Learn whose problem you are solving](#stage-1)
2. [Connect the problem to a measurable outcome](#stage-2)
3. [Test the smallest useful idea](#stage-3)
4. [Make and communicate the next product decision](#stage-4)

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Learn whose problem you are solving

**Learn:**

- User goals and context
- Observation versus assumption
- Problem statements

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).

**Practise:** Choose one learning or support service. Adapt the empathy-mapping practice to record a user task, obstacles and assumptions; discuss the task with a willing participant if available.

**Advance when:** Your problem statement names a user, a task and an observed obstacle; assumptions are labelled separately.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Connect the problem to a measurable outcome

**Learn:**

- Outcome versus feature
- Baseline measures
- Opportunity prioritisation

**Resource still needed:** A verified product-outcome and prioritisation curriculum has not yet been catalogued; the practice is an editorial exercise, not a method certified by an institution.

**Practise:** List three possible responses to the problem. Define an observable outcome, a baseline and a constraint for each, then rank the options using a stated decision rule.

**Advance when:** A reader can trace your chosen option to a user problem, outcome measure and explicit tradeoff.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Test the smallest useful idea

**Learn:**

- Prototype scope
- Experiment design
- Evidence limits

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Penpot](https://github.com/penpot/penpot) — beginner in this topic; freemium access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-penpot).

**Practise:** Build a small clickable prototype of the chosen idea. Write a neutral task, observe a consenting participant or label a self-review, and record findings without claiming production impact.

**Advance when:** You have a prototype, a repeatable task and a findings log that distinguishes observed behaviour from predicted benefit.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Make and communicate the next product decision

**Learn:**

- Success criteria
- Cost and risk
- Decision review

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Markdown Architectural Decision Records (MADR)](https://github.com/adr/madr) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-architecture.md#resource-madr).

**Practise:** Combine the brief, options, prototype findings and measures into a product decision packet. Use the decision-record structure to explain whether to continue, change or stop the idea.

**Advance when:** The packet includes a problem, evidence, decision, rejected option and a measurable next experiment; no unmeasured business result is presented as proven.

## After this recipe

Repeat with a second problem and real stakeholder feedback before taking responsibility for a product roadmap.
