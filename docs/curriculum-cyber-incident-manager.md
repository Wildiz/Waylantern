# Learning curriculum: Cyber Incident Manager

Coordinate a fictional security response while preserving evidence and separating containment from recovery.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Operations, platforms and IT support](professions-operations.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Cyber Incident Manager](professions-operations.md#cyber-incident-manager)

**Starting point:** Prior practical experience required

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Have operational incident experience and security fundamentals.
- Understand authorised evidence handling and the need for legal/privacy escalation.

**What you will produce:** A security-incident tabletop with scope decisions, evidence records, containment options and recovery criteria.

**Resource coverage:** 1 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Recognise and scope the security report

**Learn:**

- Indicators versus conclusions
- Affected assets and evidence boundaries

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [CyberDefenders](https://cyberdefenders.org/blue-team-labs/) — intermediate in this topic; freemium access. [Details](area-security.md#resource-cyberdefenders).

**Practise:** Use an available authorised defensive lab or supplied fictional logs to record indicators and possible affected assets; verify any lab charges before enrolling.

**Advance when:** Separate what the evidence shows from hypotheses and record the source and handling of each artefact.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Coordinate containment decisions

**Learn:**

- Containment trade-offs
- Evidence preservation and authority

**Resource still needed:** No reviewed full incident-response coordination, forensic preservation or legal-notification guide is catalogued.

**Practise:** For a fictional compromised account, compare disabling access with monitoring it and record who may authorise each action.

**Advance when:** The decision accounts for impact, evidence loss and privacy without giving a learner authority over real accounts.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Plan eradication and safe return

**Learn:**

- Recovery prerequisites
- Communications and notification escalation

**Resource still needed:** Current legal obligations and a complete reviewed cyber-response recovery procedure remain outside the verified catalogue.

**Practise:** Draft recovery checks, stakeholder updates and questions for the organisation's legal/privacy and technical owners.

**Advance when:** Restoration conditions include evidence that the initial access path is addressed; do not invent legal deadlines.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Capstone: run a security-response tabletop

**Learn:**

- Decision and evidence logs
- Lessons and outstanding risk

**Resource still needed:** This is an editorial tabletop, not an official response playbook or demonstrated readiness for a real security incident.

**Practise:** Work through a fictional alert, containment decision and recovery review with staged new evidence.

**Advance when:** The final record traces authority, evidence, containment, recovery checks and unresolved risks without publishing sensitive artefacts.

## After this recipe

Use current organisational response plans, legal advice and supervised defensive exercises before directing real incidents.
