# Learning curriculum: Compliance Consultant

A general compliance-advisory recipe for scoping obligations, tracing evidence and communicating limitations in a fictional technology service.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Cybersecurity, audit and risk](professions-security.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Compliance Consultant](professions-security.md#compliance-consultant)

**Starting point:** Learn the stated foundations first

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Clear analytical writing and familiarity with basic organizational processes.
- Ability to distinguish factual observations from legal or contractual interpretation.

**What you will produce:** A mock obligation register and advisory report that distinguish verified practice evidence from questions for qualified advice.

**Resource coverage:** 0 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

## Your sequence

1. [Map the organization and obligation sources](#stage-1)
2. [Build an obligation register](#stage-2)
3. [Evaluate practice against requirements](#stage-3)

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Map the organization and obligation sources

**Learn:**

- Business processes and decision owners
- Laws, contracts, policies and scope

**Resource still needed:** No jurisdiction-specific compliance course or current legal source has been verified; this role requires context beyond the catalogue.

**Practise:** Create a fictional online service and identify which stakeholders would determine its jurisdictions, contractual duties and internal policies.

**Advance when:** Your map identifies unknown applicability decisions rather than guessing legal requirements.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Build an obligation register

**Learn:**

- Exact requirement wording and interpretation
- Responsibility and evidence requests

**Resource still needed:** The clauses are editorial examples, not actual regulatory requirements or legal advice.

**Practise:** Invent three clearly labeled practice contract clauses and map each to an owner, process and possible evidence.

**Advance when:** A peer can trace each proposed test to the original fictional clause without confusing it with a law.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Evaluate practice against requirements

**Learn:**

- Evidence reliability and contradictions
- Gap severity, uncertainty and remediation

**Resource still needed:** No compliance assessment or certification methodology has been verified; conclusions apply only to the fictional exercise.

**Practise:** Review synthetic records for the fictional service, then document compliant-looking cases, gaps and inconclusive cases separately.

**Advance when:** Every conclusion states the evidence and what remains untested; proposed actions have owners.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Deliver and maintain advice

**Learn:**

- Plain-language communication
- Change tracking and reassessment triggers

**Resource still needed:** A qualified specialist must verify the actual current obligations before this workflow is used for real advice.

**Practise:** Write a bounded advisory report and a process for rechecking applicability when the service or its contracts change.

**Advance when:** The report names assumptions, open legal questions and follow-up actions without asserting real compliance.

## After this recipe

Choose an industry and jurisdiction, obtain qualified supervision and verify current primary sources before making professional compliance recommendations.
