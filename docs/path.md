## Pros and cons

<details><summary>Show pros and cons for each entry</summary>

**CS50x - Harvard's Introduction to Computer Science**

- Good: Free CS50 Certificate available (70% on each problem set and final project)
- Good: Weekly lectures with problem sets and a final project
- Good: Large community (Discord, Ed, Reddit)
- Good: Spanish and Portuguese pages linked from the site (CS50x en espanol)
- Watch out: Problem sets are demanding for complete beginners
- Watch out: Spanish and Portuguese lectures are dubbed by an AI service (lingopal.ai, per the site banner); other materials not checked in those languages
- Watch out: Verified edX certificate costs money

**From Nand to Tetris**

- Good: Ties hardware to software end to end
- Good: All lectures, projects and software tools on the site
- Good: Taught at 400+ institutions
- Watch out: Coursera version (with certificate) is paid
- Watch out: Projects take considerable time
- Watch out: Non-profit use license only

**Full Stack Open**

- Good: Free certificate and optional ECTS credits
- Good: Spanish translation plus several other languages
- Good: Continuous course with no yearly cutoff
- Good: Active Discord
- Watch out: Expects good programming skills and basic web/database knowledge
- Watch out: Large time commitment (5-14 credits)
- Watch out: Credit registration process has its own rules (see course FAQ)

**Hack The Box Academy**

- Good: Free signup gives 30 cubes, enough for the Fundamental modules
- Good: No install needed: browser-based Pwnbox
- Watch out: Easy, Medium and Hard modules need purchased cubes or a subscription
- Watch out: Certification exams cost from $490

**MIT OpenCourseWare**

- Good: No account or enrollment needed
- Good: Real MIT problem sets and exams
- Good: Downloadable materials under an open license
- Watch out: No credit, certificate or instructor feedback
- Watch out: Courses are archived snapshots - you must assemble your own path

**Open Source Society University - Computer Science**

- Good: Published inclusion criteria
- Good: Active community on Discord
- Good: Fork-and-checklist progress tracking
- Watch out: Needs strong self-discipline
- Watch out: Some graded work is paid
- Watch out: Assumes high-school math

**Open Source Society University - Data Science**

- Good: Clear sequence from intro to final project
- Good: Follows an external standard
- Watch out: Org page shows last update May 2025
- Watch out: may be slowing

**Open Source Society University - Math**

- Good: Useful math base for data science and ML
- Good: Courses chosen by published criteria
- Good: Active Discord community
- Watch out: Heavy time commitment
- Watch out: Some graded work may cost money
- Watch out: Math rather than programming

**Open Source Society University - Pre-college Math**

- Good: Clear linear order with prerequisites
- Good: Placement advice and final tests per course
- Good: Fills the maths gap before CS or data science
- Watch out: Long (weeks to months per course)
- Watch out: Mostly video-based
- Watch out: Math only

**open-source-cs**

- Good: Lists school
- Good: duration
- Good: weekly effort and prerequisites per course
- Watch out: Overlaps heavily with OSSU

**PortSwigger Web Security Academy**

- Good: 100% free interactive labs with progress tracking
- Good: Written by the Burp Suite team; regularly adds new topics
- Watch out: Assumes basic web knowledge (HTTP, HTML)
- Watch out: Labs are easier with the free Burp Suite Community Edition installed

**pwn.college**

- Good: Free and open to everyone, run by a university
- Good: Lecture videos and slides free for non-commercial use
- Watch out: Challenge solutions are asked not to be posted online
- Watch out: Steep for total beginners: assumes you are comfortable with a terminal

**Stanford Engineering Everywhere**

- Good: Complete Stanford intro CS sequence in order
- Good: Creative Commons licensed (except the Logic course)
- Good: Includes homework and exams
- Watch out: Recorded course versions have not been checked for recency
- Watch out: No grading or community support on the site

**The Odin Project**

- Good: Project-based
- Good: Active community
- Watch out: Mostly web-focused

**TryHackMe**

- Good: Beginner-friendly guided paths starting from scratch
- Good: Labs run in the browser, no setup needed
- Watch out: Has a paid Premium plan; which rooms and paths are free was not confirmed
- Watch out: Premium price not confirmed (pricing page needs JavaScript)

**USACO Guide**

- Good: Ordered modules by division (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Advanced)
- Good: Written by USACO finalists with curated problem lists
- Good: Free to read; includes an editor and graph visualizer
- Watch out: Aimed at competitive programming, not general software engineering
- Watch out: Optional live classes were promoted on the site; their price was not checked
