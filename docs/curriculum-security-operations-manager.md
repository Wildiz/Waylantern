# Learning curriculum: Security Operations Manager

A management-level recipe for coordinating monitoring and response, staffing coverage and measurable service improvement.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Cybersecurity, audit and risk](professions-security.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Security Operations Manager](professions-security.md#security-operations-manager)

**Starting point:** Prior practical experience required

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Experience handling operational security cases.
- Experience with escalation, incident coordination and team handoffs.
- Ability to discuss staffing and service responsibilities with stakeholders.

**What you will produce:** A reviewed operating plan with coverage, decision rights, quality measures and an incident exercise report.

**Resource coverage:** 0 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Review the operating model

**Learn:**

- Monitoring scope and dependencies
- Decision rights and service expectations

**Resource still needed:** No complete security-operations management curriculum has been verified; use an experienced supervisor to review the operating model.

**Practise:** Map a fictional SOC's stakeholders, data dependencies, escalation authority and out-of-hours coverage.

**Advance when:** The map distinguishes analyst decisions, managerial decisions and actions requiring business approval.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Define quality and capacity measures

**Learn:**

- Alert demand and investigation quality
- Workload, coverage and training gaps

**Resource still needed:** No verified staffing model or service-level benchmark is provided; all queue data and targets are editorial examples.

**Practise:** Create a synthetic weekly queue and compare workload, case age and review quality; identify misleading metrics.

**Advance when:** Your analysis proposes capacity changes and explains why raw closure counts cannot establish effectiveness.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Coordinate an incident exercise

**Learn:**

- Incident roles and communication cadence
- Evidence preservation and stakeholder decisions

**Resource still needed:** No incident-command curriculum or organization-approved playbook has been verified; this is a simulated coordination exercise.

**Practise:** Run a tabletop around a suspected account compromise using fictional logs, a decision log and a communications plan.

**Advance when:** The exercise records decision owners, escalation times, unresolved risks and an approved communication route.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Deliver an improvement plan

**Learn:**

- Prioritization, resourcing and accountability
- Review cycles and lessons learned

**Resource still needed:** This editorial capstone does not substitute for supervised security-operations management experience.

**Practise:** Combine the operating model, workload analysis and tabletop findings into a plan with owners and measurable review points.

**Advance when:** A reviewer can trace each proposed change to evidence, a resource assumption and a way to evaluate its outcome.

## After this recipe

Review the plan with experienced security and operations leaders; validate assumptions against a real team before adopting staffing or response targets.
