# Path: interface and product design

UX and UI designers study how people use a product and make its interfaces understandable; product designers may also connect those decisions with broader product goals. This short route introduces observation, layout, prototyping and feedback through one small project. It does not cover the whole profession or promise job readiness. Penpot is a design tool, not a complete design course, and Carbon is a reference system rather than a curriculum. You can use their documentation while practising without installing their source code.

If a step assumes programming or terminal skills you have not learned, use the relevant parts of [Path 0](path-0-fundamentals.md) first. Work through one small step at a time; you do not need to finish every linked course.

## Step 1: Understand one task before drawing screens

Pick a small task, such as finding and saving a learning resource. Write down who needs to do it, what they are trying to achieve and where an existing interface makes it difficult. If possible, ask a willing person to describe how they would approach the task; otherwise mark your assumptions clearly. Sketch two or three screens on paper or in Penpot before adding visual detail. Use fictional content rather than collecting personal information.


- [Penpot](https://github.com/penpot/penpot) (Free + paid extras): An open-source interface design and prototyping platform for practising layouts, reusable components and design systems with SVG and CSS-based workflows.

**Advance when:** You can explain the user's goal, identify one obstacle and show a sketch that addresses it.

## Step 2: Build a readable prototype for the task

Turn the sketch into a small clickable Penpot prototype. Consult Carbon for examples of buttons, form labels, spacing and component states; you do not need to implement its code or copy its branding. Keep labels clear, make text readable and avoid relying on colour alone to explain a state. Include a success screen and one error or empty state so the flow has a clear outcome.


- [Penpot](https://github.com/penpot/penpot) (Free + paid extras): An open-source interface design and prototyping platform for practising layouts, reusable components and design systems with SVG and CSS-based workflows.
- [IBM Carbon Design System](https://github.com/carbon-design-system/carbon) (Free): IBM's open-source design system with interface components, design tokens, styles and documentation for studying how a reusable product interface is built.

**Advance when:** Someone can click through the prototype from its starting screen to a visible result, and you can explain the labels and states you chose.

## Step 3: Try the prototype and improve one confusing part

Ask a willing person to try the original task without telling them which button to press. Observe where they hesitate, ask what they expected and record brief anonymous notes with their agreement. If nobody is available, walk through it yourself and label that as a self-review rather than user testing. Revise one confusing part and keep before-and-after screenshots. This is a small practice test, not evidence that the interface is accessible to every user or validated for production.


- [Penpot](https://github.com/penpot/penpot) (Free + paid extras): An open-source interface design and prototyping platform for practising layouts, reusable components and design systems with SVG and CSS-based workflows.

**Advance when:** You have a short problem statement, a clickable prototype and a note explaining one observation or self-review finding and the change it led to.
