# Learning curriculum: IT Project Manager

Coordinate a small technology project through scope, dependencies, risk and acceptance.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Leadership, product and technology relationships](professions-leadership.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [IT Project Manager](professions-leadership.md#it-project-manager)

**Starting point:** Prior practical experience required

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Has participated in a technology project and understands its delivery lifecycle.
- Can work with estimates, simple costs and stakeholder requirements.

**What you will produce:** A small project control pack with an accepted result and a review of planned versus actual work.

**Resource coverage:** 1 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

## Your sequence

1. [Define what the project is meant to achieve](#stage-1)
2. [Build a dependency-aware plan](#stage-2)

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Define what the project is meant to achieve

**Learn:**

- Business objective
- Scope boundary
- Acceptance ownership

**Resource still needed:** A verified project-management curriculum covering chartering and accountability has not yet been catalogued.

**Practise:** For a small internal-service change, write the objective, in-scope and out-of-scope work, acceptance evidence and the person empowered to accept it.

**Advance when:** The charter states a measurable objective, exclusions, decision owner and completion evidence.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Build a dependency-aware plan

**Learn:**

- Work breakdown
- Estimation uncertainty
- Resource constraints

**Resource still needed:** Verified scheduling, estimation and project-control learning material is still needed.

**Practise:** Break the scope into deliverables, identify dependencies and estimate ranges. Create a baseline plan with owners, assumptions and one explicit resource constraint.

**Advance when:** The plan shows which delay affects the completion date and explains estimate ranges rather than presenting guesses as commitments.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Control risk and proposed changes

**Learn:**

- Risk response
- Change evaluation
- Stakeholder communication

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Markdown Architectural Decision Records (MADR)](https://github.com/adr/madr) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-architecture.md#resource-madr).

**Practise:** Maintain a small risk and issue log. Use the decision-record structure to compare a proposed scope change, including cost, schedule, acceptance and stakeholder consequences.

**Advance when:** A reader can trace one risk to an owner and response, and one change to an explicit accept, defer or reject decision.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Deliver, accept and learn

**Learn:**

- Acceptance checks
- Handover
- Project review

**Resource still needed:** A verified curriculum for project closure, benefits review and delivery governance is not yet available in the catalogue.

**Practise:** Coordinate one bounded deliverable in a practice project. Gather acceptance evidence, write handover notes and compare the outcome with the plan, including what remained unfinished.

**Advance when:** The pack includes charter, plan, logs, a recorded acceptance decision, handover and a review of variance and lessons.

## After this recipe

Take a larger project only with supervision and appropriate domain knowledge; add formal project-management sources when verified.
