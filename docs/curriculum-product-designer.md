# Learning curriculum: Product Designer

Connect a product problem, a usable concept and a measurable learning question.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Software, interfaces and embedded systems](professions-software.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Product Designer](professions-software.md#product-designer)

**Starting point:** Start with the basics

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Basic computer skills
- Interest in user and product decisions

**What you will produce:** A product concept with a tested interface, trade-offs and a proposed success measure.

**Resource coverage:** 4 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

## Your sequence

1. [Frame the product problem](#stage-1)
2. [Compare solution concepts](#stage-2)
3. [Design a coherent experience](#stage-3)
4. [Deliver the product-design case](#stage-4)

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Frame the product problem

**Learn:**

- User needs
- Product constraints
- Success assumptions

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).

**Practise:** Define a fictional study-planning problem with a user, constraint and proposed outcome measure.

**Advance when:** Distinguish the problem from a preferred feature or unsupported business claim.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Compare solution concepts

**Learn:**

- Alternatives
- Value and effort
- Scope trade-offs

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).

**Practise:** Sketch three approaches including a low-technology option and choose a small testable scope.

**Advance when:** Explain how the selected concept meets the stated need and constraint.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Design a coherent experience

**Learn:**

- Interaction flow
- Visual hierarchy
- Reusable components

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Penpot](https://github.com/penpot/penpot) — beginner in this topic; freemium access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-penpot).
- [GOV.UK Design System](https://github.com/alphagov/govuk-design-system) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-govuk-design-system).

**Practise:** Prototype the concept with consistent components and explicit empty and error states.

**Advance when:** A reviewer completes the key task and identifies its primary action without coaching.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Deliver the product-design case

**Learn:**

- Prototype testing
- Decision rationale
- Measurement plan

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Open Practice Library](https://github.com/openpracticelibrary/openpracticelibrary) — intermediate in this topic; free access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-open-practice-library).
- [Penpot](https://github.com/penpot/penpot) — beginner in this topic; freemium access. [Details](area-design-product.md#resource-penpot).

**Practise:** Deliver a revised prototype, decisions and a plan for measuring the intended product outcome.

**Advance when:** Show an observation-driven change and distinguish tested usability from hypothetical product impact.

## After this recipe

Deepen research and business-context skills; a prototype does not demonstrate product-market fit or financial impact.
