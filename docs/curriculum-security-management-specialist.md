# Learning curriculum: Security Management Specialist

A governance and coordination recipe connecting policies, accountable risk treatments and measurable control reviews.

[Home](../README.md) / [Curricula](curricula.md) / [Cybersecurity, audit and risk](professions-security.md#roles-in-this-group)

**Target profile:** [Security Management Specialist](professions-security.md#security-management-specialist)

**Starting point:** Learn the stated foundations first

**Type:** Waylantern editorial learning recipe. Related official sources are listed separately below.

## Before you start

- Working knowledge of common security controls.
- Familiarity with business processes and stakeholder responsibilities.

**What you will produce:** A small security-management pack with a policy, risk-treatment register and a completed review cycle.

**Resource coverage:** 1 of 4 stages have no recorded resource gap. Read the marked gaps before planning your study.

Resource access labels refer to the material or software. Open a resource's details to check prerequisites, hardware, APIs and other running costs before using it.

<a id="stage-1"></a>
## 1. Connect security to business needs

**Learn:**

- Information ownership and business consequences
- Policy hierarchy and decision rights

**Study resources** — consult the sections needed for this task; the resources may teach complementary skills:

- [Professor Messer (CompTIA Network+ and Security+ videos)](https://www.professormesser.com/security-plus/sy0-701/sy0-701-video/sy0-701-comptia-security-plus-course/) — beginner in this topic; free access. [Details](area-devops.md#resource-professor-messer).

**Practise:** Model a fictional service and identify who owns information, operates controls and accepts risk.

**Advance when:** The responsibility map avoids making the security team the owner of every business decision.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 2](#stage-2).

<a id="stage-2"></a>
## 2. Write an implementable policy

**Learn:**

- Objectives, scope and exceptions
- Clear requirements and control ownership

**Resource still needed:** No verified security-management standard or organization-approved policy framework is supplied; this is a practice policy.

**Practise:** Draft a short editorial access-management policy with measurable requirements and an exception process.

**Advance when:** Each requirement names an accountable owner and a practical way to check implementation.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 3](#stage-3).

<a id="stage-3"></a>
## 3. Track treatments and control evidence

**Learn:**

- Risk-treatment decisions and dependencies
- Control reviews and overdue exceptions

**Resource still needed:** No formal management-system certification criteria have been verified; the register is not proof of conformity.

**Practise:** Create a synthetic register linking three risks to policy requirements, evidence checks, owners and review conditions.

**Advance when:** The register separates accepted risk, pending treatment and missing evidence rather than reporting all items as resolved.

After the checkpoint, [continue to stage 4](#stage-4).

<a id="stage-4"></a>
## 4. Run a management review

**Learn:**

- Reporting, decisions and follow-up
- Continual improvement and reassessment

**Resource still needed:** This editorial management review does not replace supervised experience or a verified professional management curriculum.

**Practise:** Hold a tabletop review of the register and policy; record decisions, resources needed and changes after one failed control check.

**Advance when:** The final pack connects the failed check to a decision and an owner, with a scheduled verification action.

## After this recipe

Ask risk owners and experienced security managers to review the pack and adapt it to an organization's real policies and obligations.
